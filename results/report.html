Protecting dependency capacity can cost successful delivery.

Synthetic portfolio demonstration · 2026-10-18

Executive summary

With a 24-slot waiting queue, outage success averages 75.4% for baseline, 74.1% for bounded retries and 70.0% for the breaker. The breaker reserves less dependency work but rejects requests that the other policies can serve. Under overload, baseline succeeds on 62.5% of arrivals; bounded retries achieve 0.7% and the breaker 9.9%. None meets the illustrative 80% success target.

Queue admission changes the conclusion. The original unbounded model showed much greater retry amplification; those runs are preserved separately. This report uses the hardened bounded model, with no policy selected as a universal winner.

The outage breaker's 0.156s terminal p95 includes immediate rejections. Its 0.725 attempts/request uses every arrival in the denominator, including requests rejected without a dependency attempt. A value below one is load shedding, not a fractional attempt or proof of greater efficiency.

Why retries can worsen successful delivery

A client timeout leaves dependency work running or queued. Retried work consumes scarce admission slots; short 0.45s attempt timeouts can expire in the queue even when the baseline's 3s timeout would succeed. Overflow ends the request immediately without retry. This comparison changes both timeout and retry settings, so it does not isolate the effect of retries alone.

The overload baseline delivers 62.5% rather than building an unlimited backlog. The breaker rejects aggressively after local failures and delivers 9.9%. Capacity protection and successful request completion are distinct objectives.

Operational decision

Validate timeout budgets, cancellation and bounded admission together before adding a breaker. Define business priority and an approved deferred/manual workflow for rejected requests. None of these numeric settings is a production recommendation, RTO commitment or compliance claim.

Model and evidence

Seeded arrivals → policy gate → six workers + 24 waiting slots → response / timeout → bounded backoff → one terminal outcome
Scenario and policy contract

120s of Poisson arrivals; response events drain afterward. Healthy/outage rate: 4/s; overload: 80/s. Healthy service: uniform 0.08–0.16s. Outage [40,70), evaluated at service start: 2s work then failure. Baseline: 3s timeout, zero retries. Bounded/breaker: 0.45s timeout, at most two retries, 0.1/0.2s backoff; total request budget 1.65s. Breaker: five consecutive observed failures, 5s cooldown, one half-open probe. Equal-time events use insertion order; initial arrivals are inserted before response events.

One shared breaker, fixed capacity, FIFO reservations and ineffective cancellation. No trained ML model, live dependency, autoscaling, jitter, distributed breaker, persistent data or measured failover. Synthetic success fractions are not uptime SLAs. Three primary and ten sensitivity seeds do not characterize every workload.
